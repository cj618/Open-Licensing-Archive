# BSD / University of California

The BSD family is central to the history of Unix licensing because it marks the transition from AT&T-encumbered academic Unix distributions to broadly reusable permissive software.

## Scope

This family covers:

- UC Berkeley Computer Systems Research Group releases;
- Regents of the University of California copyright notices;
- BSD 4-clause, 3-clause, and 2-clause variants;
- Net/2, 386BSD, FreeBSD, NetBSD, OpenBSD, and related source-tree notices;
- historically important advertising-clause and attribution-clause variants.

## Research questions

- Which historical texts are actual source-tree notices rather than later generic SPDX formulations?
- When did the advertising clause appear and disappear in particular distributions?
- Which BSD-derived projects retained UC Regents notices and which replaced them with project-specific notices?
- How did the BSD family influence later permissive licensing such as ISC, MIT/X11, PostgreSQL, and Apache-style licensing?

## Collection policy

Modern SPDX texts are useful baselines, but historical BSD entries should preserve the exact wording from source releases where possible.
